# Practice: Structure and acid strength

These are mostly "justify" questions. Write a full answer (claim, feature, and how it affects the conjugate base) before opening the solution.

---

**1.** Rank HClO, HClO₂, HClO₃, and HClO₄ from weakest to strongest acid. Justify your ranking.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄**

Each step adds an electronegative O atom bonded to Cl. The added O atoms (1) pull electron density away from the O–H bond, making the H easier to remove, and (2) spread the negative charge of the conjugate base over more O atoms by resonance. ClO₄⁻ is the most stable conjugate base, so HClO₄ ionizes the most and is the strongest acid.

</details>

---

**2.** Rank HOCl, HOBr, and HOI from strongest to weakest acid. Justify your ranking.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**HOCl > HOBr > HOI**

All three have the same H–O–X structure. Cl is the most electronegative of the three halogens, so it pulls the most electron density away from the O–H bond and best stabilizes the negative charge on OCl⁻. Weaker pull (Br, then I) means a less stable conjugate base and a weaker acid.

</details>

---

**3.** Trichloroacetic acid (Cl₃CCOOH) has a much larger Ka than acetic acid (CH₃COOH). Explain this difference in terms of molecular structure.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

In Cl₃CCOOH, three H atoms on the carbon next to the –COOH group are replaced by highly electronegative Cl atoms. Through the **inductive effect**, the Cl atoms pull electron density toward themselves, away from the carboxylate group. This spreads out and stabilizes the negative charge on Cl₃CCOO⁻. A more stable conjugate base means a greater extent of ionization and a larger Ka.

</details>

---

**4.** Propanoic acid has the formula CH₃CH₂COOH. How many acidic hydrogens does it have?

(A) 1  (B) 2  (C) 5  (D) 6

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**(A).** Only the H in the –COOH group is acidic, because it's bonded to an electronegative O and its conjugate base is resonance-stabilized. The five C–H hydrogens are not acidic.

</details>

---

**5.** Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) and acetic acid (CH₃COOH) both contain an O–H group, yet acetic acid's Ka is roughly 10¹¹ times larger. Explain why, with reference to the conjugate bases.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

In acetate (CH₃COO⁻), the negative charge is **delocalized by resonance** over two equivalent O atoms. In ethoxide (CH₃CH₂O⁻), the charge is localized on a single O atom, and there's no resonance. Spreading the charge makes acetate far more stable, so acetic acid gives up H⁺ much more readily.

</details>

---

**6.** Rank ClO⁻, ClO₂⁻, and ClO₄⁻ from strongest to weakest base.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**ClO⁻ > ClO₂⁻ > ClO₄⁻**

The rankings for conjugate bases run opposite to their acids. HClO is the weakest acid, so ClO⁻ is the strongest base. HClO₄ is a strong acid, so ClO₄⁻ is essentially not basic at all. (Ka × Kb = Kw.)

</details>

---

**7.** Fluorine is more electronegative than chlorine, yet HF is a weak acid and HCl is a strong acid. Explain.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Going **down a group**, atom size, not electronegativity, controls the trend. F is a very small atom, so the H–F bond is short and very strong, and harder to break than the longer, weaker H–Cl bond. Cl⁻ is also larger than F⁻, so its negative charge is spread over a bigger volume, which makes it more stable. Both factors make HCl ionize completely while HF ionizes only partially.

</details>

---

**8.** Phosphorous acid, H₃PO₃, gives a titration curve with only **two** equivalence points when titrated with NaOH. Its structure is shown below. Explain why it behaves as a diprotic acid even though it has three H atoms.

```
        O
        ‖
  H – P – O – H
        |
        O – H
```

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Only H atoms bonded to **O** are acidic. Two of the H atoms are in O–H groups, which are polar bonds whose conjugate base is stabilized by the electronegative O atoms. The third H is bonded directly to P. The P–H bond is much less polar, and removing that H doesn't give a stabilized conjugate base, so it doesn't ionize. Two acidic H atoms give two equivalence points.

</details>

---

**9.** Methylamine, CH₃NH₂, is a weak base.

(a) Write the equation for its reaction with water.

(b) Which atom in CH₃NH₂ accepts the proton? Explain using its structure.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

(a) CH₃NH₂(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)

(b) **Nitrogen.** N has a **lone pair** of electrons that can form a bond to H⁺. This is the same feature that makes NH₃ a base. The C and H atoms have no lone pairs.

</details>
