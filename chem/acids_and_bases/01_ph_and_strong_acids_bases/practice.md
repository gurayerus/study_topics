# Practice: pH and strong acids and bases

Assume 25 °C unless a problem says otherwise. Try each problem before opening the solution.

---

**1.** Solution A has a pH of 2.0 and solution B has a pH of 5.0. What is [H₃O⁺] in A divided by [H₃O⁺] in B?

(A) 0.4  (B) 2.5  (C) 3  (D) 1000

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**(D).** Each pH unit is a factor of 10, and the pH values differ by 3 units, so the ratio is 10³ = 1000. Choices (A)–(C) come from treating pH as if it were linear.

</details>

---

**2.** A solution has [H₃O⁺] = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ M. Calculate its pH, pOH, and [OH⁻].

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

```
pH    = −log(3.2 × 10⁻⁴) = 3.49
pOH   = 14.00 − 3.49    = 10.51
[OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ = 3.1 × 10⁻¹¹ M
```

The concentration has 2 sig figs, so the pH gets 2 decimal places.

</details>

---

**3.** Calculate the pH of 0.0040 M Sr(OH)₂.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Sr(OH)₂ is a group 2 hydroxide, a strong base that releases **2** OH⁻ per formula unit.

```
[OH⁻] = 2 × 0.0040 = 0.0080 M
pOH   = −log(0.0080) = 2.10
pH    = 14.00 − 2.10 = 11.90
```

</details>

---

**4.** 25.0 mL of 0.200 M HCl is diluted with water to a total volume of 500.0 mL. What is the pH of the diluted solution?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

```
mol HCl = (0.0250 L)(0.200 M) = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol
[H₃O⁺]  = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol / 0.5000 L = 0.0100 M
pH      = −log(0.0100) = 2.000
```

You can also use M₁V₁ = M₂V₂. The concentration has 3 sig figs, so the pH gets 3 decimal places.

</details>

---

**5.** A student dissolves 0.56 g of KOH (molar mass 56.11 g/mol) in enough water to make 250.0 mL of solution. What is the pH?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

```
mol KOH = 0.56 g / 56.11 g/mol = 9.98 × 10⁻³ mol
[OH⁻]   = 9.98 × 10⁻³ mol / 0.2500 L = 0.040 M
pOH     = −log(0.040) = 1.40
pH      = 14.00 − 1.40 = 12.60
```

</details>

**6.** Conjugate pairs.

(a) In the reaction NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq), identify the acid, the base, and both conjugate pairs.

(b) Write the formula of the conjugate acid and the conjugate base of HCO₃⁻.

(c) What is the term for a species like HCO₃⁻ that can act as either an acid or a base?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

(a) H₂O is the acid (it donates H⁺) and NH₃ is the base (it accepts H⁺). The conjugate pairs are NH₃/NH₄⁺ and H₂O/OH⁻.

(b) The conjugate acid adds H⁺ to give **H₂CO₃**. The conjugate base removes H⁺ to give **CO₃²⁻**.

(c) **Amphoteric** (some textbooks say amphiprotic).

</details>

**7.** At 60 °C, Kw = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴.

(a) Calculate the pH of pure water at 60 °C.

(b) Is pure water at 60 °C acidic, basic, or neutral? Justify your answer.

(c) Is the autoionization of water endothermic or exothermic? Justify your answer using the Kw values at 25 °C and 60 °C.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

(a) In pure water [H₃O⁺] = [OH⁻] = x, so x² = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴.

```
x  = √(9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 3.1 × 10⁻⁷ M
pH = −log(3.1 × 10⁻⁷) = 6.51
```

(b) **Neutral.** Each H₂O that ionizes produces one H₃O⁺ and one OH⁻, so [H₃O⁺] = [OH⁻] still holds. Neutral means equal concentrations, not pH 7.

(c) **Endothermic.** K increased from 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ to 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ as temperature rose. For an endothermic reaction, heat acts like a reactant, so raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium toward products and increases K.

</details>

**8.** Which of the following solutions has the highest pH?

(A) 0.010 M HCl  (B) 0.010 M NaOH  (C) 0.010 M Ba(OH)₂  (D) 0.015 M KOH

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**(C).** Compare [OH⁻]: (B) 0.010 M, (C) 2 × 0.010 = 0.020 M, (D) 0.015 M. The highest [OH⁻] gives the highest pH. For (C), pOH = 1.70, so pH = 12.30.

</details>

---

**9.** Which is closest to the pH of 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ M HCl?

(A) 8.00  (B) 7.00  (C) 6.98  (D) 6.00

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**(C).** An acid solution can't have a pH above 7, so (A) is a trap. The HCl adds only 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ M H₃O⁺, which is small next to the roughly 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ M that water supplies itself. Together they give [H₃O⁺] ≈ 1.05 × 10⁻⁷ M, so the pH is just below 7. You won't need the exact calculation on the AP exam, but you should recognize that the answer must be slightly below 7.

</details>

**10. (Free-response style)** A student has two beakers: one with 0.10 M HCl and one with 0.10 M CH₃COOH.

(a) Which solution has the lower pH? Justify your answer in terms of the species present in each solution.

(b) The student claims that the two solutions would need the same volume of 0.10 M NaOH to neutralize them. Do you agree? Explain.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

(a) **HCl has the lower pH.** HCl is a strong acid and ionizes completely, so [H₃O⁺] = 0.10 M (pH 1.00). CH₃COOH is a weak acid. Only a small fraction of its molecules ionize, so most remain as CH₃COOH and [H₃O⁺] is much less than 0.10 M. Lower [H₃O⁺] means higher pH. (You'll calculate it in folder 2. It's about 2.87.)

(b) **Agree.** Both solutions contain the same number of moles of acid that can donate a proton. As OH⁻ removes H₃O⁺, the weak acid keeps ionizing (Le Châtelier) until all of it has reacted. Neutralization depends on **moles of acid**, not on how strong the acid is.

</details>
